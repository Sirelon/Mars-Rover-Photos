# Photo feed & data sources

Read before touching paging, `NasaApi`, a rover's photo source, or the Viking catalogue.

The heart of the app is the shared rover photo feed in `shared/.../data/paging/`: an app-singleton `RoverFeedPager` exposes one cached `PagingData` stream collected by both the list grid and the fullscreen viewer, with a per-rover `FeedMode` seam — sol-keyed (`SolPagingSource`: Curiosity, Perseverance, Ingenuity, InSight, Viking 1, Viking 2, Phoenix) vs page-keyed with a random anchor (`ImagesSearchPagingSource`: Spirit, Opportunity). The class KDocs there document the non-obvious invariants (continuation pages, anchor resolution, write-through cache merge rules, the offline fallback to cached photos) — read them before touching paging. Data-source constraints: the classic `api.nasa.gov/mars-photos` API is permanently dead; Spirit/Opportunity only exist in the curated images.nasa.gov library (no raw archive API); each rover's source is mapped in the repository/network layer.

Two things about the Mars 2020 raw-image feed (`mars.nasa.gov/rss/api`), which serves both Perseverance and Ingenuity, are worth knowing before you touch `NasaApi`. First, it does not resolve a duplicated query parameter as "last wins" — it discards both and falls back to its own default. `category` and `order` are therefore passed as parameters and must never be reintroduced into the URL literal: a second `category` silently answers an Ingenuity request with Perseverance photos, which then cache under the wrong rover id. Second, the sol filter is `condition_3=<sol>:sol:in`; the `sol=<n>:sol:in` form looks equivalent but is ignored on an empty sol, returning the entire unfiltered feed instead.

Earth dates from the raw feeds are timestamps, not dates. The Curiosity feed (`mars.nasa.gov/api/v1/raw_image_items`) has no `earth_date`: read `date_taken`, never `created_at`, which is when NASA stored the record and can be years after the sol. The Mars 2020 feed reports `date_taken_utc` without a zone suffix. `MarsImage.earthDate` keeps the timestamp so the photo info sheet can show the time; anything that shows only a date goes through `formatDisplayDate`, which takes the date part.

Ingenuity is also the app's one genuinely sparse sol feed: 14,553 images spread over 162 sols, running from sol 43 — the day it was set down on the surface, its first images long predating its first flight — to sol 1069, with the largest gap running 76 sols. A uniform random anchor lands on an empty sol six times out of seven, and `SolPagingSource` resolves those by probing forward one network request per sol. `PhotosViewModel.randomize()` therefore asks `PhotosRepository.nearestSolWithPhotosAtOrAfter` for a photo-bearing sol first, which the feed's undocumented `gte` range operator answers in a single request. That lookup is Ingenuity-only and falls back to the plain scan on any failure; `SolPagingSource` itself is deliberately untouched by it.

The two Viking landers are the exception to "the source is an API": their 1976-1982 archive is a static PDS file tree with nothing to query, so the app ships the index. `scripts/generate-viking-catalog.mjs` turns the PDS `index.tab` volumes into the two `composeResources/files/viking*_catalog.txt` resources — verifying every derived image URL against the live archive before it writes — and `data/viking/VikingCatalog` parses them behind `PhotosRepository`. Because the catalogue carries a real sol per photo, Viking reuses the sol feed unchanged. The generator also drops what is not worth looking at: frames narrower than 100px (`MIN_BROWSABLE_WIDTH`) and a short list of blank diagnostics (`NON_PHOTOGRAPHIC_NOTES`). The Viking cameras were facsimile scanners that built an image one 512-pixel column at a time while turning, so width is purely how far the camera swept, and the sub-100px band is entirely optical-depth readings and calibration rather than photographs. That is why the seeded `totalPhotos` (1333 / 1581) is far smaller than the archive (3542 / 3043). Regenerate with the script; never hand-edit the resources.

The Phoenix lander (2008) follows the Viking pattern: `scripts/generate-phoenix-catalog.mjs` builds `composeResources/files/phoenix_catalog.txt` from the PDS index volumes of the Surface Stereo Imager and the Robotic Arm Camera, verifies every derived browse URL against the live archive, and `data/phoenix/PhoenixCatalog` serves it behind `PhotosRepository`. The archive holds 32,115 frames from those two cameras and the catalogue keeps 1,924. Most of the rest are not pictures: the stereo imager's windowed frames are sun-tracking and wind-sock crops, its 4x1-summed and 4x4-decimated frames are sky studies, and the solar-filter, flat-field and reference-pixel products are calibration. Browse JPEGs under 12 KB (8 KB for the arm camera's half-height frames) are blank, so the listing's file size drops them. Phoenix also photographed each scene through several filters and both stereo eyes within a minute or two; the generator keeps the largest JPEG of each such run, so the feed shows each view once instead of four to fifteen times. The optical-microscope volume is left out. The archive server answers bursts of parallel requests with HTTP 500, so the generator fetches four listings at a time and retries.

"A day on Mars" (`DayOnMarsRepositoryImpl`) asks every mission for the photos taken on one Earth date through `PhotosRepository.photosTakenOn`, and never through a sol estimate. A UTC date overlaps one or two sols, and `RoverDateUtil.solFromDate` is often one or two sols off for the landers, so a window around the estimate misses real photos. Both raw feeds filter by shot time server-side with a range in their from/to condition slots: `condition_2=<date>:date_taken:gte` and `condition_3=<next date>:date_taken:lt`, compared in UTC. The Curiosity/InSight feed answers with `total`, the Mars 2020 feed (Perseverance, Ingenuity) with `total_results` (its `total_images` is the whole feed). One request returns at most 100 full frames, so a busy date is a sample plus its total. The Viking and Phoenix catalogues answer from the date on each row. Spirit and Opportunity cannot answer: images.nasa.gov dates them by press release, not by shot.
